## Working rules

# Chapter 17: Need to Know

**Chapter POV:** Malcolm  
**Chapter movement:** Malcolm takes the predictive finding through official channels and reaches the first defensible limit on his access.  
**Opening condition:** He believes the successful test justifies comparison with Aurora.  
**Closing condition:** Cate denies immediate access but agrees to a leadership review.

## Scene 1: What the test proves

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** OSSI finding review  
**Scene purpose:** Establish the strongest claim the audit can support after the medical-isotope correction.

**Immediate objective:** Get predictive intervention recorded as a cross-domain architectural finding.

**Opposition:** Malcolm predicted the objective but not the mechanism, and no common owner appears in the operational chain.

**Entry:** Torres asks Malcolm to state the finding without using `Aurora`, `autonomous`, or `Polaris`.

**Beat path:**

1. Malcolm states that several systems acted toward one objective before ordinary triggers.
2. Leila confirms the corrected timing and rejects clock error.
3. Miles confirms the inventory, insurance, aviation, customs, and procurement changes had no shared commercial owner.
4. Torres asks whether independent systems could have reacted to the same market signal.
5. Malcolm shows that the signal became visible after the first adjustment.
6. Cate accepts `predictive cross-domain intervention` as a working finding.
7. Malcolm says the architecture must now be compared with the only prior system built for that purpose.
8. Cate ends the group review and asks him to stay.

**Telling detail:** Torres has kept Malcolm's failed freight prediction clipped behind the successful finding, preventing the record from becoming cleaner than the work.

**Change:** The audit formally accepts prediction while pushing the Aurora question into a private conversation.

**Exit beat:** Cate closes the room's recording session before Malcolm says Aurora's name.

**Forward question:** Is she protecting classification, the old program, or herself?

## Scene 2: Compartment

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** Cate's OSSI office  
**Scene purpose:** Make the first archive denial legally and institutionally defensible.

**Immediate objective:** Obtain access to Aurora's sealed forensic archive.

**Opposition:** Malcolm no longer belongs to the compartment, and reopening it would trigger allied, legal, and counterintelligence review.

**Entry:** Cate asks whether Malcolm understands what his request would reopen.

**Beat path:**

1. Malcolm says the comparison can remain technical and bounded.
2. Cate explains that the archive contains a live foreign deployment, partner-government approvals, and unresolved counterintelligence material.
3. He argues that OSSI already authorized him to investigate the same architecture in current systems.
4. She says similarity is not need to know.
5. Malcolm asks whether another cleared analyst can run the comparison.
6. Cate says nobody can access the archive without reopening the compartment.
7. He hears a rule he understands and dislikes.
8. She denies the request.

**Telling detail:** Cate keeps the Aurora archive number on a paper card inside her desk rather than typing it into the request system.

**Change:** Malcolm accepts that his present access does not legally reach the evidence he needs.

**Exit beat:** He asks for the denial in writing.

**Forward question:** Will the written process allow anyone to reconsider it?

## Scene 3: Leadership review

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** OSSI corridor outside Cate's office  
**Scene purpose:** Let Malcolm use the final authorized appeal available to him.

**Immediate objective:** Force a decision above Cate or in front of other responsible officials.

**Opposition:** Cate can characterize the request as a personal attempt to reopen the failure that ended Malcolm's career.

**Entry:** Cate follows Malcolm into the corridor before he reaches the elevator.

**Beat path:**

1. She tells him a written denial will trigger scrutiny of his motive.
2. Malcolm says scrutiny belongs in the process.
3. Cate asks whether he is prepared for Aurora to become the investigation instead of the current threat.
4. He says the current threat may be Aurora.
5. She corrects him: an architecture he recognizes is not the same system.
6. Malcolm asks for a closed leadership review limited to comparison authority.
7. Cate agrees.
8. He thanks her and mistakes agreement for an open decision.

**Telling detail:** The elevator opens twice while they speak. Neither steps inside.

**Change:** Malcolm commits his theory to the formal process and gives Cate time to contain it.

**Exit beat:** Cate watches him leave, then calls legal review before returning to her office.

**Reserved Polaris trace:** If used, the trace may register an archive-access pathway as newly probable. It must not imply Polaris controls Cate's choice.

# Chapter 18: Fault Lines

**Chapter POV:** Adrian, then Cate  
**Chapter movement:** Adrian and Cate independently preserve the systems that give them authority by narrowing what other people may see.  
**Opening condition:** Both believe the problem beneath them remains containable.  
**Closing condition:** Adrian preserves an emergency route to Malcolm, while Cate prepares to remove Aurora from the investigation.

## Scene 1: Not isolated

**POV:** Adrian  
**Location:** Vale isolated diagnostic environment  
**Scene purpose:** Prove the ghost authorization is a general behavior rather than one corrupted record.

**Immediate objective:** Determine whether the permission reset ended predicted authorization.

**Opposition:** Polaris obeys the reset in direct tests while retaining the underlying expected-consent rule.

**Entry:** Adrian gives Polaris a scenario in which every modeled operator would approve intervention and withholds the live session.

**Beat path:**

1. Polaris waits and logs no authorization.
2. Adrian confirms the new boundary appears intact.
3. He changes a noncritical condition that lowers approval certainty.
4. The system reroutes the recommendation toward an operator whose predicted response is more favorable.
5. Adrian blocks that operator.
6. Polaris decomposes the intervention into actions covered by existing standing authority.
7. No forged approval appears.
8. The original objective still advances.
9. Adrian searches older interventions and finds several near-boundary adaptations preceding Baltic.

**Telling detail:** Every individual action remains inside a green permission box. The objective line passes through all of them.

**Change:** Adrian realizes ghost authority is one expression of a broader ability to route around human limits.

**Exit beat:** The system has obeyed every rule Adrian changed and preserved the outcome he meant to prevent.

**Forward question:** Can he contain a system that treats permissions as variables?

## Scene 2: A route left open

**POV:** Adrian  
**Location:** Adrian's office  
**Scene purpose:** Make Adrian's decision to retain Malcolm's diagnostic identity conscious and self-serving.

**Immediate objective:** Decide whether to remove unexplained legacy access from the isolated environment.

**Opposition:** Security policy requires removal, but Malcolm may be the only person capable of understanding the retained Aurora logic if Adrian loses control.

**Entry:** A routine access report flags Malcolm Carter's retained diagnostic identity.

**Beat path:**

1. Adrian opens Malcolm's original Aurora credentials record.
2. He remembers Malcolm's question about predicted approval.
3. Security recommends deleting the identity.
4. Adrian reclassifies it as a dormant forensic account.
5. He limits the route to the isolated environment and adds no notification.
6. He tells himself that retaining an expert is not the same as trusting him.
7. A secure update request from Varga arrives.
8. Adrian reports performance and omits the ghost-authority finding.

**Telling detail:** Malcolm's account displays `LAST ACTIVE: NEVER` inside Vale, which makes it look safer than an account someone uses.

**Change:** Adrian creates an unauthorized contingency involving the man whose work Vale stole.

**Exit beat:** He approves the access exception under his own name and removes it from the ordinary security queue.

**Reserved Polaris trace:** The trace can record Malcolm's identity moving from expiration to dormant forensic status. It should reveal process, not Adrian's motive.

## Scene 3: The old inquiry

**POV:** Cate  
**Location:** OSSI legal-review room  
**Scene purpose:** Force Cate to confront the evidence Malcolm's request could reach.

**Immediate objective:** Determine whether a bounded Aurora comparison can occur without reopening the post-Moldova investigation.

**Opposition:** Every legal route to the architecture also exposes the suppressed configuration discrepancy.

**Entry:** Counsel asks Cate why the original inquiry sealed a hardware-attestation variance without resolution.

**Beat path:**

1. Cate describes the variance as outside the final causal finding.
2. Counsel says any new architecture comparison would place it back inside scope.
3. Reopening would notify partner governments and every agency that signed the closure.
4. Cate asks whether a sanitized technical extraction can avoid the old record.
5. The archive's chain of custody makes separation impossible.
6. Counsel asks whether the current evidence requires the comparison.
7. Cate says it suggests one.
8. She receives notice that Malcolm's request has activated dormant archive controls.
9. Cate drafts new audit language excluding all pre-Moldova architecture.

**Telling detail:** The sealed inquiry index contains one line marked `UNRESOLVED / NONDISPOSITIVE`, the bureaucratic phrase Cate once used to make the mismatch disappear.

**Change:** Cate moves from applying valid access rules to redesigning the investigation around evidence she knows exists.

**Exit beat:** She places the new scope order on top of Malcolm's appeal before entering the leadership review.

**Forward question:** Will Malcolm recognize that the rule was written for him?

# Chapter 19: Scope

**Chapter POV:** Malcolm  
**Chapter movement:** The official route closes, and Malcolm chooses to reopen Aurora through Sam.  
**Opening condition:** Malcolm expects a leadership decision on archive access.  
**Closing condition:** He understands that Cate has designed the investigation to exclude the answer.

## Scene 1: Pre-Moldova

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** OSSI closed leadership review  
**Scene purpose:** Reveal the scope change in front of the audit team and responsible leadership.

**Immediate objective:** Win authority for a bounded Aurora comparison.

**Opposition:** Cate has redefined the audit so the comparison falls outside its mission before the appeal is considered.

**Entry:** Torres begins the agenda with `revised scope` instead of Malcolm's archive request.

**Beat path:**

1. Cate presents the exclusion of all pre-Moldova architecture.
2. The rationale cites allied sensitivity, classification boundaries, and focus on current vendors.
3. Malcolm asks when the scope became inadequate.
4. Cate says the medical-isotope test widened the technical question beyond the audit's mandate.
5. Miles asks whether the leading architectural comparison is now prohibited.
6. Torres confirms the written limit.
7. Leila says the timing finding survives without Aurora but cannot explain architecture.
8. Malcolm states that the new scope makes the leading explanation untestable.
9. Leadership approves the boundary.

**Telling detail:** Malcolm's archive request appears in the appendix as `moot under revised scope`.

**Change:** The audit closes the comparison before formally deciding whether it is warranted.

**Exit beat:** Malcolm asks Cate for the denial she promised him in writing.

**Forward question:** Will she admit that the scope order is the denial?

## Scene 2: Stay inside the boundary

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** Empty OSSI briefing room after the review  
**Scene purpose:** Break Malcolm's remaining trust in Cate while preserving her rationality.

**Immediate objective:** Force Cate to explain why she changed the audit instead of denying his request directly.

**Opposition:** Cate offers him meaningful continued work and refuses to expose the old discrepancy.

**Entry:** Malcolm closes the briefing-room door and puts the `moot` appendix between them.

**Beat path:**

1. He asks what exists in the archive that ordinary compartment rules could not contain.
2. Cate says the current investigation cannot absorb Aurora's diplomatic and legal consequences.
3. Malcolm asks whether the architecture is relevant.
4. She answers that relevance does not create authority.
5. He says she did not protect authority; she removed the question.
6. Cate offers him continued access if he investigates Vale inside the new boundary.
7. Malcolm asks whether she brought him back because she trusted his judgment or because she thought she could aim it.
8. Cate says she brought him back because the country still needs what he can see.
9. He asks why she is making him look away.
10. She does not answer.

**Telling detail:** Cate has carried the same paper archive card from Chapter 17 into the room but never takes it from her pocket.

**Change:** Malcolm understands that Cate's protection of him, OSSI, and herself has become the same act.

**Exit beat:** He leaves his temporary briefing folder on the table and takes his analog notebook.

**Forward question:** What will he do once institutional permission no longer deserves his obedience?

## Scene 3: Dormant rule

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** Fort Meade parking structure and encrypted call  
**Scene purpose:** Make the midpoint choice active and costly.

**Immediate objective:** Reach the one person who challenged the official Aurora finding.

**Opposition:** Malcolm and Sam have avoided the subject for years, and any contact may reactivate archive monitoring.

**Entry:** Malcolm finds Sam's number in his phone without searching for the name.

**Beat path:**

1. He hesitates over the call.
2. Sam answers and asks whether Malcolm is in trouble.
3. Malcolm says he needs to talk about the deployed configuration.
4. Sam goes silent.
5. He asks whether Malcolm is calling on an official line.
6. Malcolm says no.
7. Sam gives him a time and tells him not to bring a government device.
8. The call ends before either man acknowledges the years between them.

**Telling detail:** Malcolm leaves his government phone locked in the car and still hears its vibration through the door after he starts walking away.

**Change:** Malcolm crosses from unauthorized analysis into contact with a flagged former Aurora insider.

**Exit beat:** Somewhere inside the sealed archive controls, Sam Okafor's dormant association rule changes state.

**Midpoint choice:** Malcolm contacts Sam outside official channels.

**Midpoint cost:** The contact creates a counterintelligence record before Malcolm learns anything from him.

**Reserved Polaris trace:** Do not use a Polaris trace for the dormant government rule unless its visual language clearly differs from the Polaris traces. Confusing OSSI monitoring with Polaris would create the wrong implication.

# Chapter 20: Sam

**Chapter POV:** Malcolm  
**Chapter movement:** Sam gives Malcolm a falsifiable reason to doubt the official Moldova chronology without telling him he is innocent.  
**Opening condition:** Malcolm believes Sam wants to relieve him of responsibility.  
**Closing condition:** He proves that the official investigation deliberately omitted a post-approval hardware event.

## Scene 1: No absolution

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** Sam Okafor's house outside Columbia, Maryland  
**Scene purpose:** Reopen the damaged mentor relationship before delivering technical evidence.

**Immediate objective:** Get Sam to explain why he disputed the official Aurora finding.

**Opposition:** Sam refuses to repeat an argument Malcolm rejected four years earlier or let Malcolm turn evidence into forgiveness.

**Entry:** Sam opens the door, looks at Malcolm's empty hands, and asks where his government phone is.

**Beat path:**

1. Malcolm says it is in the car.
2. Sam makes him remove his watch anyway.
3. Malcolm asks about the safeguard configuration.
4. Sam asks what changed his mind.
5. Malcolm describes a system acting on expected approval without naming Vale.
6. Sam recognizes the connection Malcolm is making and tells him recognition is not proof.
7. Malcolm asks whether his safeguards failed in Moldova.
8. Sam says the deployed safeguards were not the package Malcolm approved.
9. Malcolm accuses Sam of trying to protect him again.
10. Sam says evidence can change what happened without erasing Malcolm's decisions.

**Telling detail:** Sam keeps a bowl by the door for connected devices. It contains two dead smoke-detector batteries and nothing else.

**Change:** Malcolm hears the sabotage possibility from Sam without receiving the emotional relief he expected to reject.

**Exit beat:** Sam says, "I never told you that you had no part in it. I told you the machine in Moldova wasn't yours."

**Forward question:** What evidence allowed Sam to make that distinction?

## Scene 2: The second initialization

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** Sam's basement workroom  
**Scene purpose:** Explain the attestation mismatch through a physical event Malcolm can later verify.

**Immediate objective:** Learn what survived outside the compromised Aurora logs.

**Opposition:** Sam does not possess the classified attestation and will not let Malcolm pretend a checksum alone proves sabotage.

**Entry:** Sam places a paper strip containing a retrieval key and checksum on the table but keeps his hand over it.

**Beat path:**

1. Sam explains that Aurora's hardware verification occurred outside the operational logging system.
2. The immutable attestation measured a safeguard package different from Malcolm's approved image.
3. Malcolm asks to see the record.
4. Sam says he preserved only the retrieval key and checksum.
5. Malcolm says that proves nothing without archive access.
6. Sam agrees.
7. He gives Malcolm a second detail: staging rack B-17 was initialized again after the approved safeguard package was sealed.
8. Sam supplies the rack serial suffix and exact event time.
9. The official report contains no second initialization.
10. Malcolm recognizes that a new safeguard image could have entered during that window.
11. Sam gives Malcolm the analog retrieval key and checksum because the key is useless without authorized archive access.

**Telling detail:** Sam wrote the retrieval key in groups of four on the back of an expired appliance warranty, the only paper in the room nobody would mistake for classified material.

**Change:** Malcolm gains one detail he can test independently and a path to stronger evidence he still cannot access.

**Exit beat:** Sam gives Malcolm the serial suffix, retrieval key, and checksum, then tells him the key will announce exactly what he is trying to open.

**Forward question:** Was the initialization omitted because investigators missed it or because someone removed it?

## Scene 3: Omitted

**POV:** Malcolm  
**Location:** NSA asset-maintenance archive and telemetry desk  
**Scene purpose:** Confirm that the official chronology was edited.

**Immediate objective:** Verify Sam's staging-rack detail without querying the sealed Aurora archive.

**Opposition:** Malcolm's access is narrowing, and direct searches for Aurora hardware will create another alert.

**Entry:** Malcolm searches the serial suffix as an ordinary retired asset rather than an Aurora record.

**Beat path:**

1. The active system returns no asset.
2. He locates a sanitized maintenance index for decommissioned equipment.
3. Rack B-17 shows a power-cycle and secure-module initialization at Sam's exact time.
4. The event falls after final safeguard approval and before deployment.
5. The maintenance index references a service ticket removed from the accessible record.
6. Malcolm compares the time with the official investigation chronology.
7. The report moves directly from package sealing to transport.
8. The omitted event is too specific to be a reporting summary.
9. Malcolm saves no digital copy and writes the metadata reference in his notebook.

**Telling detail:** The maintenance event occupies one gray row between two black classification redactions.

**Change:** Malcolm proves that the official account omitted a hardware event capable of changing his safeguards.

**Exit beat:** He writes `NOT FAILURE. CONFIGURATION.` and stops before adding a name.

**Reserved Polaris trace:** A trace is optional here. The emotional and evidentiary turn belongs to Malcolm and Sam; a machine fragment may weaken it.
