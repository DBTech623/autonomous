# Pilot — Character-Introduction Pattern Variation

Source: line editorial review's recurring-pattern note ("A number of supporting characters enter through a similar compressed template: approximate age, race/skin tone, hair, build/clothes, then a behavioral inference... I would vary the method") plus 11 individual Strong-tier notes (LE-011, 017, 027, 030, 031, 040, 041, 068, 078, 079, 126).

**Ground rule for this pass:** vary structure and technique, not content — every character keeps their heritage/physical-identity marker somewhere in the passage. That grounding was deliberate work done earlier in this project (see `Character_Physical_Descriptions.md`); the reviewer's notes ask for variation in *how* it's delivered, never for cutting it. Techniques used below: action-first, one memorable detail instead of a stacked list, folding description into an existing action clause, and description via another character's memory (Reddick) rather than direct observation.

**Sharper finding beyond the reviewer's individual notes:** four separate characters (Cate, Zhou, Zeynep, Reddick) use the near-identical construction "stopped [verb]-ing it, [time] ago" for a hair/headscarf detail. First grep pass only caught three (missed Reddick's "stopped needing it to be" — different verb, same construction); caught on self-audit after re-checking with a broader pattern. This is a harder repetition than general template fatigue and is fixed distinctly in all four below.

Once approved, fold into manuscript, reader-notes, and the correct Movement file per character (to be verified by exact-text grep at fold-in time, same as prior passes).

## 1. Naomi Kincaid (Ch2) — technique: let the comic payoff land first

BEFORE: `Naomi Kincaid said it from the doorway, thirty-two years old, brown skin, dark hair pulled back and already losing the fight it always lost by afternoon, which meant everyone in the metrics meeting had time to look guilty before she reached the table.`

AFTER: `Naomi Kincaid said it from the doorway, and everyone in the metrics meeting had time to look guilty before she reached the table. Thirty-two, brown skin, dark hair already losing the fight it always lost by afternoon.`

## 2. Lauren Beck (Ch3) — technique: one image instead of a stacked list

BEFORE: `She did not sit. Beck never sat at an analyst's station. White, lean and efficient, the kind of build that looked like it was already leaving a room even when she'd just arrived; she kept her hair short for the same reason she never sat down — one less thing to manage. She rested two fingers on the divider, leaned far enough to read the display, and kept the rest of herself pointed toward wherever she had intended to be.`

AFTER: `She did not sit. Beck never sat at an analyst's station. White, lean, hair kept short because it was one less thing to manage. She rested two fingers on the divider, leaned far enough to read the display, and kept the rest of herself pointed toward wherever she had intended to be.`
(Revised after user flagged that my first draft's "already angled toward the door before she'd finished arriving" duplicated the idea already carried by the kept closing line "pointed toward wherever she had intended to be" — two images doing the same job back to back. Swapped in the hair/efficiency detail from the original instead of inventing a second version of the same image.)

## 3. Cate Mercer (Ch5) — technique: fold into the bookending action; fix the shared phrase

BEFORE: `On the other side of the turnstile, Cate Mercer waited with one hand resting over the other. Late fifties, gray hair cut short enough that she'd clearly stopped thinking about it as a decision twenty years ago, dressed like the room she was about to walk into always mattered. She had watched both attempts without stepping closer.`

AFTER: `On the other side of the turnstile, Cate Mercer waited with one hand resting over the other, gray hair cut short and practical. She had watched both attempts without stepping closer, dressed like the room she was about to walk into always mattered.`
(Self-audit catch: "unfussed-over" was an awkward coinage, not real usage. Swapped for "practical.")

## 4. Dr. Leila Haddad (Ch5) — technique: action first, physical detail trails

BEFORE: `Dr. Leila Haddad, according to the nameplate. CISA. Olive-skinned, dark hair in a low knot already coming loose at one side, the kind of small quick hands that never stopped annotating something. She kept working, having already divided his timeline into three separate windows and moved them to opposite sides of the wall.`

AFTER: `Dr. Leila Haddad, according to the nameplate. CISA. She'd already split his timeline into three windows and moved them to opposite corners of the wall. Olive-skinned, dark hair coming loose from its knot, the kind of quick hands that never stopped annotating something.`
(Revised after user asked what "before he'd finished reading the door" referred to — nothing; I'd invented a detail with no basis in the scene, which actually has Cate opening the door, Malcolm walking through it, a scene break, and only then his "Where's the overlay?" question before Leila's introduction. Cut the fabricated clause rather than replace it with another invented image.)

## 5. Gabriel Torres (Ch5) — technique: fewer, sharper details, folded into the handshake

BEFORE: `Gabriel Torres rose and offered Malcolm a hand. Broad through the shoulders, brown-skinned, salt-and-pepper hair cut close, the patient posture of someone accustomed to waiting beside cargo that had missed a connection.`

AFTER: `Gabriel Torres rose and offered Malcolm a hand, broad through the shoulders, brown-skinned.`
(Revised after user flagged that "the patient posture of someone accustomed to waiting beside cargo that had missed a connection" has read as unmoored across multiple prior reads. Checked: it isn't tied to anything else established about Torres anywhere in the book — no shipping/logistics backstory, nothing — it's a floating simile that predates even the heritage pass. Cut rather than replaced, per user's call; his established temperament — meticulous, dry, "moves and talks at exactly one speed" — already comes through in his dialogue in the same scene without needing a supporting image here.)

## 6. Adrian Vale (Ch6) — technique: trim and de-duplicate, not compress (major-character entrance)

BEFORE: `Adrian Vale crossed the floor without the cluster of aides Malcolm associated with chief executives, a single slate under one arm, screen dark. Mid-to-late forties, pale in the way of someone who spent most of his life under building light rather than daylight, dark hair going gray at the edges in a way that looked intentional even though nothing about him suggested he'd arranged it. Analysts didn't look up as he passed — not fear, Malcolm thought, just people used to him being there.`

AFTER: `Adrian Vale crossed the floor without the cluster of aides Malcolm associated with chief executives, a single slate under one arm, screen dark. Mid-to-late forties, pale from a life spent mostly under building light instead of daylight, dark hair graying at the edges like he'd arranged it, though nothing else about him suggested he arranged anything. Analysts didn't look up as he passed. Not fear, Malcolm thought, just people used to him being there.`
(Removes the duplicate "in a way... in a way" construction; keeps full detail since this is a major-character entrance, not a supporting-cast beat. Self-audit catch: my first draft's "planned anything by accident" was a logical contradiction — planning and accident cancel each other out. Fixed to "arranged... arranged anything," which keeps the original's actual point: the hair looks deliberate, but nothing else about him suggests he's someone who curates his appearance. Also split the pre-existing em-dash in "he passed — not fear" into two sentences — that dash predates this edit, but since the paragraph was already open, fixed it per the standing minimize-em-dashes preference.)

## 7. Dr. Elara Zhou (Ch6) — technique: distinct phrasing to break the shared construction

BEFORE: `A woman was already seated at the far end, laptop open where everyone else had a slate — mid-forties, dark hair pulled back with the kind of efficiency that suggested she'd stopped treating it as a choice years ago, sleeves already pushed up as though the meeting were beside the point.`

AFTER: `A woman was already seated at the far end, laptop open where everyone else had a slate, sleeves already pushed up as though the meeting were beside the point. Mid-forties, dark hair pulled back hard enough to mean business.`

## 8. Marcus Reddick (Ch11) — technique: keep the ringtone/memory device, trim the list

BEFORE: `The name had its own ringtone, one of only four in the building that did. Adrian didn't need to look at the readout to picture him — fifty, white, gray hair kept military-short decades after he'd stopped needing it to be, built solidly without ever dressing like a man who wanted anyone to notice.`

AFTER: `The name had its own ringtone, one of only four in the building that did. Adrian didn't need to look at the readout to picture him: fifty, white, hair buzzed short out of habit more than requirement, built like a man who'd spent decades making sure nobody noticed him.`
(Self-audit catch: this is actually a **fourth** instance of the "stopped [verb]-ing it, [time] ago" construction shared with Cate/Zhou/Zeynep — my original grep pattern was too narrow to catch "stopped needing it to be," and my first-draft fix preserved it almost verbatim as "stopped needing it that way." Rewritten with "out of habit more than requirement" to actually break the pattern this time.)

## 9. Zeynep Acar (Ch13) — technique: distinct phrasing to break the shared construction

BEFORE: `Zeynep Acar checked Naomi's name against the appointment list, headscarf plain and dark and tied tight enough that she'd clearly stopped adjusting it hours ago, moving like she was already late for whatever came next.`

AFTER: `Zeynep Acar checked Naomi's name against the appointment list without looking up, headscarf plain and dark, already half a step into whatever came next.`

## 10. Elif Karaca (Ch13) — technique: integrate into the seated-at-the-table image instead of a standalone fragment

BEFORE: `Naomi looked past her. Elif Karaca sat at the end of a crowded table with three tenants, two children, and a municipal inspection report. The parliamentary office occupied the first floor of a converted storefront. Posters covered one wall. The other held shelves of legal binders, donated diapers, and bottled water.

Early forties, olive-skinned, dark hair cut short with one streak of gray at the temple she'd earned faster than she should have. She was dressed in a suit chosen for how many hours it could survive rather than how it photographed.`

AFTER: `Naomi looked past her. Elif Karaca sat at the end of a crowded table with three tenants, two children, and a municipal inspection report. Early forties, olive-skinned, one streak of premature gray at her temple, dressed in a suit chosen for how many hours it could survive rather than how it photographed. The parliamentary office occupied the first floor of a converted storefront. Posters covered one wall. The other held shelves of legal binders, donated diapers, and bottled water.`
(Self-audit gap, caught by user: my first draft used an em-dash to join the table-contents clause to her description. Split into two sentences instead — also avoids the description blending into the comma-heavy list of what's on the table.)

## 11. Mara Voss (Ch23) — technique: one detail folded into the action, drop the redundant age stat

BEFORE: `At 6:42, a woman crossed the lobby carrying a leather portfolio — mid-forties, fair-skinned, ash-blond hair cut for speed rather than style. She saw Naomi, changed direction without breaking stride, and stopped beside the empty chair.`

AFTER: `At 6:42, a woman crossed the lobby carrying a leather portfolio, fair-skinned, ash-blond hair cut for speed rather than style. She saw Naomi, changed direction without breaking stride, and stopped beside the empty chair.`
