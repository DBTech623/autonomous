# Pilot — Strong Tier, Chapters 6–10

Source: line editorial review, Strong-tier items falling in Ch6–10 (16 items, including LE-058 which was deferred from the explain-after-image pass as not actually that pattern).

---

## 1. Ch6 — "walking the table" (LE-038)

BEFORE: `Leila kept walking the table with her finger, asking the kind of questions that made rehearsed demonstrations come apart at the seams.`

AFTER: `Leila kept tracing the table with her finger, asking the kind of questions that made rehearsed demonstrations come apart at the seams.`

**What changed:** swapped "walking" for "tracing" — the original verb drew attention to itself without adding meaning; "tracing" is the more natural verb for a finger moving across a display.

## 2. Ch6 — repeated "answer" (LE-042)

BEFORE: `He wondered, not for the first time that day, what he would have built if he'd said yes — and disliked that some part of him already knew the answer, and that the answer looked like this floor.`

AFTER: `He wondered, not for the first time that day, what he would have built if he'd said yes, and disliked that some part of him already knew the answer, and that it looked like this floor.`

**What changed:** cut the second "the answer," replaced with "it." Also removed the em-dash (swapped for a comma) since the sentence was already being touched.

## 3. Ch6 — the card reveal (LE-043)

BEFORE: `He took a card from his jacket and set it on the table, then turned it face up himself: a name, a title, one line for a number that wasn't the switchboard.`

AFTER: `He took a card from his jacket and set it face up on the table: a name, a title, one line for a number that wasn't the switchboard.`

**What changed:** collapsed "set it down, then turned it face up" (two mechanical steps) into one motion — "set it face up on the table."

## 4. Ch6 — dropped clause (LE-044)

BEFORE: `"The provenance holds," Leila said, watching the numbers descend. "Whether there's a shared objective, he says there isn't."`

AFTER: `"The provenance holds," Leila said, watching the numbers descend. "As for a shared objective, he says there isn't one."`

**What changed:** the original read like a dropped clause. Recast per the reviewer's own suggested wording.

## 5. Ch6 — "seen him decide to" (LE-045)

BEFORE: `"His slate lit up twice in twenty minutes," Leila said. "He read it exactly once, right after making sure we'd both seen him decide to."`

AFTER: `"His slate lit up twice in twenty minutes," Leila said. "He read it exactly once, right after making sure we'd both seen him decide to read it."`

**What changed:** added "read it" — the dangling "decide to" made the reader backtrack to figure out what he'd decided to do.

## 6. Ch6 — elevator direction (LE-046)

BEFORE: `On the seventeenth floor, Adrian watched the elevator car cross from twelve to the lobby on a screen no bigger than his palm, then set the slate face-down on his desk without waiting for the doors to open.`

AFTER: `On the seventeenth floor, Adrian watched the elevator car descend from twelve to the lobby on a screen no bigger than his palm, then set the slate face-down on his desk without waiting for the doors to open.`

**What changed:** "cross" → "descend." An elevator between floors descends, it doesn't cross.

## 7. Ch7 — opening sentence (LE-047)

BEFORE: `Vale's technical-review floor sat two levels under the atrium everyone photographed for the annual report, where the noise of open-plan optimism didn't reach — just server intake fans, a vending machine that had given up on everything but a single dented energy drink, and forty screens running quiet audits nobody upstairs would ever read.`

AFTER: `Vale's technical-review floor sat two levels under the atrium everyone photographed for the annual report. The noise of open-plan optimism didn't reach down here — just server intake fans, a vending machine that had given up on everything but a single dented energy drink, and forty screens running quiet audits nobody upstairs would ever read.`

**What changed:** split the chapter's opening sentence in two so the reader gets oriented (where we are) before absorbing the sensory list (what it's like), instead of holding both in one breath. Kept the remaining em-dash — the sensory list is still a legitimate parenthetical run, not a candidate for a second cut in the same sentence.

## 8. Ch7 — the triage/CEO-queue paragraph (LE-048)

BEFORE: `Normal triage read it exactly as designed: low severity, clean outcome, and a routing rule that had nothing to do with rank. It matched the affected system to its listed owner, and the orchestration layer's owner of record wasn't a department or a title. It was Adrian Vale, by name, on the asset sheet. The ticket went where the sheet said it went — past security response, straight into a technical-review queue that happened to belong to the CEO.`

AFTER: `Normal triage read it exactly as designed: low severity, clean outcome, and a routing rule that had nothing to do with rank. It matched the affected system to its listed owner: not a department, not a title, but Adrian Vale, by name, on the asset sheet.

The ticket went where the sheet said it went, past security response, straight into a technical-review queue that happened to belong to the CEO.`

**What changed:** removed the repeated "owner" ("listed owner" / "owner of record") by folding the reveal into one clause, and split the chain-of-command explanation from its consequence into two paragraphs. Also dropped the em-dash before "past security response."

## 9. Ch8 — the distribution-graph paragraph (LE-050)

BEFORE: `Malcolm returned to the article's section on distribution — a second story, folded inside this one, about what had happened to her first. Naomi had included the referral graph from that first Baltic story. Traffic rose when the outage began, leveled, and then fell off a hard edge four minutes before the port announced recovery. She had placed public indicators beneath it: search interest, link shares, two news aggregators, and the timestamp of the port statement.`

AFTER: `Malcolm returned to the article's section on distribution — a second story, folded inside this one, about what had happened to her first.

Naomi had included the referral graph from that first Baltic story. Traffic rose when the outage began, leveled, and then fell off a hard edge four minutes before the port announced recovery. She had placed public indicators beneath it: search interest, link shares, two news aggregators, and the timestamp of the port statement.`

**What changed:** pure paragraph break, separating Malcolm's framing of what this section is (a second story, about suppression) from the concrete evidence itself. Kept the first em-dash — it's doing real appositive work and a comma would be harder to parse in an already comma-dense sentence.

## 10. Ch8 — Naomi's introduction at Union Market (LE-051)

BEFORE: `Naomi sat across from him and placed her phone inside an empty tea tin. She moved like someone who used to run competitively, and had the kind of unstudied, disarming looks that made the man at the next table lose his sentence mid-order. Malcolm noticed it the way he noticed everything else that wasn't useful yet, filed it, and didn't look at it again.`

AFTER: `Naomi sat across from him and placed her phone inside an empty tea tin. She moved like someone who used to run competitively, the kind of unstudied, disarming presence that made people forget what they'd been about to say. Malcolm noticed it the way he noticed everything else that wasn't useful yet, filed it, and didn't look at it again.`

**What changed:** cut "the man at the next table" as the delivery vehicle for her "disarming" quality — that briefly shifted out of Malcolm's close POV into an anonymous bystander's reaction, which isn't a device the book uses elsewhere. Generalized to "made people forget what they'd been about to say" so the observation still lands, then let Malcolm's own noticing (already the very next clause) carry the POV instead. Checked reader-notes first: no existing annotation objects to this construction, and this placement (Ch8, tied to Malcolm's noticing-and-moving-past-it) was deliberate from earlier work — this fix reinforces that intent rather than fighting it.

## 11. Ch8 — the shielded phone (LE-053)

BEFORE: `Malcolm watched the tea tin. Her phone was inside it, still capable of doing almost anything a phone did.`

AFTER: `Malcolm watched the tea tin. Shielded or not, the phone inside it could still do almost everything a phone did.`

**What changed:** stated the practical limitation directly (shielding doesn't fully disable a phone) instead of the more roundabout original phrasing.

## 12. Ch9 — the transit/crowd paragraph (LE-056)

BEFORE: `A second monitor held the city's public transit feed, open since Luka's stream first froze. Two bus routes diverted from the counting center, rerouted for street congestion nobody had announced yet. Seven minutes later a location-sharing map lit up with two crowds moving toward the same three blocks from opposite directions, each one close to a thousand people: one carrying the stolen-ballot chant she'd heard on Luka's stream, the other answering the message that had told citizens to protect the vote from the opposition.`

AFTER: `A second monitor held the city's public transit feed, open since Luka's stream first froze. Two bus routes diverted from the counting center, rerouted for street congestion nobody had announced yet.

Seven minutes later a location-sharing map lit up: two crowds moving toward the same three blocks from opposite directions, each one close to a thousand people. One carried the stolen-ballot chant she'd heard on Luka's stream. The other answered the message that had told citizens to protect the vote from the opposition.`

**What changed:** split one long sentence carrying transit behavior, crowd movement, and both crowds' identities into four shorter beats across two paragraphs, so each piece of the pressure sequence registers on its own.

## 13. Ch9 — "close the last few blocks" (LE-057)

BEFORE: `Naomi watched the two markers on the map, waiting for them to close the last few blocks between each other.`

AFTER: `Naomi watched the two markers on the map, waiting for them to close the remaining blocks between them.`

**What changed:** cleaner idiom, less self-conscious phrasing.

## 14. Ch9 — "grew past a headcount well below" (LE-058)

BEFORE: `The largest of the two crowds never grew past a headcount well below what local police cited for emergency restrictions.`

AFTER: `The largest of the two crowds never approached the headcount local police cited for emergency restrictions.`

**What changed:** "grew past" and "well below" were duplicating the same relationship (both saying "stayed under"). One direct comparison now carries it.

## 15. Ch9 — "the only thing that had actually changed" (LE-059)

BEFORE: `Malcolm thought of the exact moment the viewer count had stopped: eighteen thousand four hundred and forty-two, Luka's hand still raised toward the officer, the live indicator still glowing over a picture that had already stopped moving. Nobody outside that room had gotten to see what happened after the count froze. That was the only thing that had actually changed.`

AFTER: `Malcolm thought of the exact moment the viewer count had stopped: eighteen thousand four hundred and forty-two, Luka's hand still raised toward the officer, the live indicator still glowing over a picture that had already stopped moving. Nobody outside that room had gotten to see what happened after the count froze. The only thing that had actually changed was who could see what happened next.`

**What changed:** specified what "the only thing" refers to — visibility, not the event itself. The preceding paragraph has several moving variables, so "that" was making the reader pause on a key inference.

## 16. Ch10 — the folder reveal (LE-062)

BEFORE: `Naomi didn't reach for the pen. She reached for the folder, the one with the Vale amendment paperclipped to a nine-year-old patent filing and a cached newsletter page nobody had thought to scrub. She'd written the name in it herself, three names down from two ordinary ones, and under it: *means nothing yet.*`

AFTER: `Naomi didn't reach for the pen. She reached for the folder: the one with the Vale amendment paperclipped to a nine-year-old patent filing and a cached newsletter page nobody had thought to scrub.

She'd written the name in it herself, three names down from two ordinary ones. Under it: *means nothing yet.*`

**What changed:** split into two paragraphs so the handwritten "means nothing yet" line lands as its own beat instead of trailing off the end of a longer sentence.

## 17. Ch10 — the OSSI disclosure (LE-063)

BEFORE: `"I work for OSSI," he said, like he'd decided to keep going before she asked what that had to do with a fellowship that never sent a follow-up.`

AFTER: `"I work for OSSI," he said, before she could ask what that had to do with a fellowship that never sent a follow-up.`

**What changed:** cut "like he'd decided to keep going" — a layer of narration describing his conversational tactic that wasn't needed on top of the disclosure itself.
