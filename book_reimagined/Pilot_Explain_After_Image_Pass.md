# Pilot — "Explain-After-the-Image" Global Pass

Source: line editorial review's recurring-pattern note ("The manuscript often produces a strong image, inference, or line of dialogue and then explains what it means... In a thriller, trusting the first beat usually increases authority and pace") plus every individual note tagged Over-explanation, Redundancy, or Foreshadowing restraint (LE-010, 012, 024, 054, 058, 064, 071, 090, 107, 111, 117, 121, 122, 125, 138, 156).

**Method:** the reviewer's own test — "whenever a strong line is followed by a sentence beginning to interpret it, test the scene with that explanatory sentence removed." Applied to each candidate below. All fixes are subtractive (cutting a trailing clause/sentence), not rewrites, which is lower-risk than the character-introduction pass — no new prose to fabricate or contradict.

**Three exceptions found on verification, not applied:**
- **LE-111 (Ch20, Sidorin's "walking on his own legs" metaphor)** — the review suggests trimming this as over-explanation, but checked the next paragraph and it's a deliberate callback: "Arslan believed the legs were his. He let himself believe it..." directly reuses the phrase. Cutting the setup would orphan the payoff. Not applying this one.
- **LE-117 (Ch22, "protecting an old decision from a new question")** — the current manuscript already ends the beat exactly where the reviewer wants it to; no trailing explanation exists to cut. Already fine, no change needed.
- **LE-012 (Ch2, "He heard himself")** — self-audit catch: a pure cut leaves two same-speaker quotes butted directly together with nothing between them (`"...coming." "That sounded..."`), which isn't just a redundant-content problem — the phrase was also doing a structural job, separating two things the same speaker says. Checked the whole manuscript for this bare quote-quote pattern with no dialogue tag or beat between: zero existing instances. Introducing it here risks reading as a merged-paragraph typo, not a deliberate device. Not applying this one.

**LE-058 excluded from this pass** (Ch9, "never grew past a headcount well below") — tagged Redundancy but it's a single-sentence internal word redundancy, not the beat-then-explanation pattern this pass targets. Left for the general Strong-tier chapter-by-chapter pass instead.

Once approved, fold into manuscript, reader-notes, and the correct Movement file per item (verified by exact-text grep at fold-in time).

AFTER: `Adrian would read four lines about a contained exposure and a firm already retained for exactly this purpose. He would not ask what "recovery" meant in practice.`
