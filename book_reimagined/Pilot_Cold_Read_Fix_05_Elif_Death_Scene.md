## What changed and why

1. **Moved Tunalı's watching beat to open the sequence instead of interrupting it.** His line "as the column's fourth vehicle crossed into the dead zone" now flows directly into "the tunnel swallowed the light" — the external view of the vehicle losing signal, then the internal experience of that same loss, back to back instead of separated. This removes one hard cut from Elif's final stretch without cutting any content — everything from both original fragments survives, just reordered.

2. **Merged the tunnel-entry beat and the brake-lights/death beat into one continuous Elif passage.** In the original, the cross-cut to Tunalı sat directly between "Comforting" and the brake lights collapsing — so the reader's last sustained time with Elif before the manuscript's report-favorite "the girl colored one window blue" line was interrupted by a scene change. Now the reader stays with her, uninterrupted, from the tunnel entrance through her last thought.

3. **Added new lines to her final moment**, before the existing "girl colored one window blue" line — revised twice after feedback:
   - First revision: a Ch13 callback was too distant to land; replaced with the "Answer when I call" / "I always answer" exchange from earlier in this *same* chapter (line ~8068–8074), which also sets up a quiet irony against the next fragment, where Naomi calls three times with no answer.
   - Second revision: the bigger gap was craft, not distance — nothing in the original sensory sequence (brake lights, a weapon, fake maintenance workers) actually tells Elif *this is fatal and unstoppable* rather than just dangerous. Added one inferential sentence connecting a detail already on the page (the radio going silent) to what it specifically means: the timing is too precise to be coincidence, which means her own detail's channel was deliberately controlled — the difference between "this is an ambush I might survive" and "this was already decided." That inference, not a new visual, is what earns her certainty and the calm that follows it.
   - Third revision: the original phrasing of that sentence ("A silent one, at the exact moment..., was not") buried the point in interrupted syntax and ended on a bare negation. Rewrote as two plain declaratives.
   - Fourth revision: "a jammed radio was an accident" presumed Elif could diagnose a specific technical cause (jamming) she has no way to know. Rewrote to reason from a protocol expectation instead — a trained detail should be talking through an obvious anomaly, and isn't.
   - Fifth revision: "They should have been talking by now. Whatever kept them quiet, someone else had decided it" was crafted, epigrammatic — the kind of line written in hindsight, not a flash thought under direct threat. Cut to "Nobody should have gone quiet like this."
   - Sixth revision: even that was still a *reasoned judgment* — Elif consciously evaluating a norm violation. The actual gap was categorical, not verbal: real danger-recognition arrives as instinct before it arrives as thought (the brain processes threat faster than conscious reasoning catches up to it). Replaced the reasoned line with a bodily/instinctive one: "She was afraid before she could have said why." The fear comes first, unexplained; the reasoning (Naomi's calls, the girl and the window) follows after, which is the actual order threat-perception happens in, not the reverse.
   - Seventh revision: added one more beat between the instinctive-fear line and the Naomi thought, to bridge "afraid before she could say why" to the more composed resignation that follows — her conscious mind catching up to the concrete cues (the men, the driver's hand) already established in the two paragraphs above, in short fragments matching the speed of the realization rather than a composed sentence: "Then the men. Then the driver's hand. Her mind was only now catching up to what her body had already known."

4. **Added two sentences immediately after the detonation**, still from Tunalı's POV (no new POV introduced), before the cut to the sanitized "blast alert" bureaucratic language. Deliberately non-graphic — sound and smoke, not injury — and it creates a beat of the real event landing before the reader gets the institution's flattened version of it, which is a small thematic echo of the book's whole argument about language obscuring what actually happened.
