# Techno-Thriller Craft Research (Reconstructed)

**Note on this document:** the original version of this research was done earlier in the same session that produced the restructuring plan, and was never saved to a file — it lived only in conversation and didn't survive summarization. This is a fresh pass, not a verbatim recovery. The three items marked **DONE** below match what actually made it into the adopted outline (`00_Reimagined_Outline.md`, Take Four); the rest are reconstructed from craft research plus a fresh read of where the current draft still runs procedural rather than propulsive. Treat the list as "probably close to the original intent," not "recovered exactly," and adjust freely.

## Research basis

- Crichton's techno-thriller formula: take a real area of science, ask what happens when human ambition outpaces human judgment, populate the disaster with specialists who understand the problem but can't control it, and never let the pace stop. Sharp scene-level pacing and cliffhanger chapter endings are load-bearing, not decorative.
- AI-as-antagonist craft: the technology needs to read as an active, consistent presence with its own "methods" (even if not a personality in the human sense) — otherwise it functions as a plot device rather than a force the characters are actually up against. Uncertainty about *who or what* is deciding is a legitimate suspense engine in its own right (identity/agency doubt), separate from uncertainty about *what happens next*.

## The seven

1. **Tighter thread intercutting** — fix stretches where a POV thread (Naomi, especially old Ch5–7) disappears for too long, breaking the parallel-jeopardy engine multiple threads are supposed to provide. **DONE** — Take Four lever 1, implemented as II.2 (the new Naomi gap scene) plus resequencing across Movements II–III.

2. **Compress repeated scene shapes** — the "question → institutional deflection → sharper question" pattern was recurring often enough (old Ch11/12/17/19/23) to flatten tension on repetition instead of building it. **DONE** — Take Four lever 2, applied across Movement III.

3. **Deepen felt cost early (Aurora atmosphere)** — give the reader physical/behavioral access to what Aurora cost Malcolm well before the Ch20/23/24 reveal ladder, without leaking any information he doesn't yet have himself. **DONE** — Take Four lever 3, texture beats in I.3, I.6, II.1, and others.

4. **Give Polaris a more live, forefront presence** — right now the system is known entirely through aftermath: logs, trace blocks, argument about what it must have done. A techno-thriller antagonist usually gets *some* on-page immediacy — not necessarily a "voice," but moments where its action is happening in real time, in scene, rather than being reconstructed after the fact. **NOT DONE.** This is the gap you flagged. Discussed below.

5. **An explicit overarching ticking clock** — Istanbul (Movement IV) already has a hard deadline and it works. The earlier movements don't have an equivalent felt deadline — the audit has urgency but not a stated clock. Worth checking whether one should be surfaced earlier (even just naming *when* the audit's findings are due, or when the next Polaris-run system goes live) so tension has something to count down against outside the climax. **NOT DONE / not evaluated in Take Four.**

6. **Cliffhanger chapter endings** — Crichton's chapters rarely end at rest. Worth an audit pass of the new 34-chapter breaks (now that they exist) specifically for whether each chapter break lands on a held breath rather than a settled beat. This is a byproduct check we haven't done — the chapter breaks were placed by a mechanical heuristic (where old chapter boundaries fall), not by where tension peaks. **NOT DONE / not evaluated.**

7. **Lean into agency-uncertainty as its own suspense engine** — the book already asks "who authorized this?" as a recurring line, which is close to this technique, but it's currently used mostly as a rhetorical beat within scenes rather than a structural device the reader is explicitly tracking across the whole book. Could be sharpened without adding new plot facts. **PARTIALLY PRESENT, not deliberately built as a structural throughline.**

## On #4 specifically, since that's what prompted this

Making Polaris more live/forefront is a real craft lever, but it's also the one with the highest risk of colliding with what's already working: the system's opacity is currently load-bearing (it's *why* "who's actually deciding" lands as dread rather than exposition). Two ways to do it without breaking that:

- **Diegetic-only presence, expanded:** more trace-block/system-readout material *in the moment* rather than reconstructed afterward — i.e., the reader sees a correction happening in real time (as raw system output) before a character explains it, rather than always after. Low risk to canon and voice; mostly a sequencing change within scenes that already have trace blocks.
- **Cross-thread visibility:** let the reader (not the characters) see the same Polaris action land in two different threads close together — e.g., a trace block in Malcolm's scene, then within a page or two, its effect surfacing in Naomi's or Ewa's thread, before either character thread would know to look for it. This is achievable with the existing intercutting work already done in Movements I–III; it's an additional editorial pass on top of it, not a new lever.

Both are compatible with the hard constraint (no new plot facts, no character-fate changes) — they're presentation-order changes, same category as the intercutting/compression work already done.
