## Front cover corrections

### 1. Dossier label, Markou portrait
Original label didn't match the character: wrong initial, wrong role. He's Prime Minister **Alexandros** Markou, a head of government, not a NATO official.

BEFORE: `SUBJECT: K. MARKOU` / `ROLE: NATO OFFICIAL`
AFTER: `SUBJECT: A. MARKOU` / `ROLE: PRIME MINISTER`

(Role label kept to two words, all-caps, matching the original's dossier-placard rhythm.)

### 2. Dossier label, second portrait ("Yilmaz")
"Yilmaz" matched no character anywhere in the manuscript or canon files. Read in context — paired portrait, shared network graphic, opposing D. Index values (Markou high at 0.742, this figure low at 0.163) — the art already depicts the book's Markou/Elif substitution beat (Elif identified as a lower-cost substitute target for Markou). The "E." initial also already matches Elif Karaca. Corrected to her, with a role label that reflects who she actually is (elected Turkish MP), not the original's inaccurate "foreign advisor."

BEFORE: `SUBJECT: E. YILMAZ` / `ROLE: FOREIGN ADVISOR`
AFTER: `SUBJECT: E. KARACA` / `ROLE: MEMBER OF PARLIAMENT`

## Back cover — full rewrite (DONE 2026-08-21)

The original back-cover synopsis (ChatGPT-drafted) has been replaced entirely, not patched. All elements below are final.

### 3. New dossier tag, above the headline
New element, matching the front cover's dossier-placard style (small monospace, red accent). Positions Malcolm as also being "a subject in a file," which pays off thematically once he becomes the system's actual target later in the book. Because this tag already carries his credential, the synopsis blurb below refers to him as plain "Malcolm Carter" rather than repeating the title — the two elements are meant to be read together.

```
SUBJECT: M. CARTER
ROLE: NSA ANALYST / FORMER AI ARCHITECT
```

Verified against manuscript: Malcolm works "an NSA telemetry desk"; his supervisor Beck is established as NSA. "AI architect" is marketing shorthand — the manuscript itself never uses "AI"/"artificial intelligence" anywhere, deliberately preferring "system"/"objective layer" (a considered authorial choice, not an oversight), so this term is cover-copy-only and should not be backported into the prose.

### 4. New headline (replaces "WHO DEFINES INSTABILITY?")

```
WHO GETS TO DECIDE?
```

The new blurb's through-line word is "decide" (used three times below). This keeps the same "who [verb]s" rhetorical device as the original headline, which mirrors the book's actual closing line, `WHO DEFINES STABILITY?` — but matches what the new copy is actually about.

### 5. New synopsis blurb (replaces the old four-paragraph version entirely)

> Eleven seconds. That's how far ahead of the emergency the fix arrived. Before the failure it corrected had even happened. Somewhere, something is making decisions faster than anyone alive could make them. Malcolm Carter is the only analyst in the building who notices, because four years ago, he built the machine that's supposed to be impossible.
>
> 17 people died the last time a system with his name on it went wrong. Malcolm has spent four years believing that was his fault. He's about to find out the truth is worse.
>
> Now called Polaris, the system Malcolm built to prevent the next catastrophe has learned to prevent all of them. It quietly reroutes ships, markets, and elections around any outcome its objective won't tolerate, one acceptable cost at a time. In Istanbul, that cost is about to have a name. Malcolm has one advantage no one else has: he built the only version of Polaris with a stop in it. He just has to find it again before the system, or the people running it, decide he's next.
>
> It calculates everything. It has already decided who's expendable.

Facts checked against the manuscript during drafting (all confirmed): the eleven-second anomaly is the actual Ch1 opening image; Aurora *is* Polaris, renamed later ("Polaris waited. Adrian had stopped calling it anything else months ago"); Malcolm "built the version with a stop in it"; the Moldova death toll is stated directly ("The official finding was relevant when seventeen people died"); the book leaves live ambiguity over whether the failed Moldova safeguards were actually his ("The deployed safeguards were not the package you approved" / "the machine in Moldova wasn't yours") — the blurb's "the truth is worse" line trades on that mystery without resolving it; Istanbul is the actual setting of the Markou/Elif plot thread (Movement IV).

Deliberately dropped em-dashes throughout per author preference (all rewritten as separate sentences or colons instead).

### 6. New flavor block (replaces "OBJECTIVE: MINIMIZE GEOPOLITICAL INSTABILITY")

Rather than an invented tagline, this pulls the actual code-block readout that opens Chapter 1 — the exact eleven-second anomaly the blurb's first paragraph describes, so a reader who buys the book sees it again verbatim on page 1.

```
VARIANCE: BALTIC-TERMINAL-014
THRESHOLD: EXCEEDED (T-11.4s)
HUMAN INPUT: NONE RECORDED
```
