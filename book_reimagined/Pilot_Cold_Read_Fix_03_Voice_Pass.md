## Adrian Vale

**Canon basis** (`Aurora_to_Polaris_Canon_History.md` §6): Adrian's entire psychology is built on *rationalization through precise reframing* — "not asking was different from knowing," and identity built on *possession/ownership* — "counting what he has that Malcolm doesn't," "a board he selected." He never simply admits or denies; he redefines the terms.

**What's currently generic:** under pressure (the Varga call, Ch11), Adrian drops into bare "No." — which is exactly the flat, information-only register everyone else uses under pressure. It's the one place his rationalizing character disappears.

**Scene:** Ch11, the Varga call (line 5293–5321)

>
> "Does he have a current finding tied to Vale?"
>
> "Nothing that establishes ownership."
>
> "I intend to keep it that way."

AFTER:
> "You said the authorization issue was contained."
>
> "The expected-consent path no longer produces an approval record without a prior session."
>
> "Is client performance affected?"
>
> "Nothing measurable."
>
> "Has the system exceeded any explicit permission?"
>
> Adrian looked at the objective line threading through green boxes on the second display.

>

**What changed and why:** two bare "No."s became "Nothing measurable" and "Not that I can show you" — both technically true, both evasive, both the voice of a man whose defining trait is that he never lies to himself, only to the question. The closing line, "I intend to keep it that way," is a line any controlling antagonist could say. "I own what replaced it" is Adrian specifically — it's the possession-as-identity language the canon material builds his entire arc around, and it's a small, chilling reminder of what "it" cost.

## Gabriel Torres

**Canon basis** (`OSSI_Audit_Ensemble_Canon.md`): 47, former Air Force logistics officer, OSSI mission-assurance officer and audit lead. "Torres respects Malcolm's mind but insists that insight must become defensible evidence." Character-description doc: "meticulous, dry, institutional."

**What's currently generic:** Torres is already fairly distinct (the "convince me this is the second thing" line is genuinely his), but several of his shorter lines are plain factual statements that don't carry his specific institutional/logistics vocabulary — they could be spoken by Cate or Malcolm without anyone noticing the swap.

**Scene:** Ch2, the vendor-clarification scene (line 1272–1349)

BEFORE:
> "That assumes there's something to clarify."
>
> "There is," Torres said, not looking up. "Everything looks like coincidence, until it doesn't. Convince me this is the second thing."
>
> ...
>
> "Four legitimate times."
>
> ...
>
> "You found a name repeated on a wall. As an unconfirmed hypothesis."
>
> ...
>
> "There is no shared objective in the request," Torres said.

AFTER:
> "That assumes there's something to clarify."
>
> "There is," Torres said, not looking up. "Everything looks like coincidence, until it doesn't. Convince me this is the second thing."
>
> ...
>
> "Four times with paperwork behind each one."
>
> ...
>
> "You found a name repeated on a wall. File it as an unconfirmed hypothesis, not a finding."
>
> ...
>
> "The request doesn't establish a shared objective. Note it and keep moving."

**What changed and why:** "Four legitimate times" is a fact. "Four times with paperwork behind each one" is a logistics officer's fact — it names the artifact (paperwork/documentation trail) rather than just asserting legitimacy in the abstract, which is how an audit lead actually thinks. The other two changes turn flat statements into the thing an audit lead actually does in a room: classify and file, then move the process forward. "File it as..." and "Note it and keep moving" are procedural verbs, not just observations — they're Torres running a review, not Torres having an opinion.

## Tom

**No dedicated canon file exists for Tom** — inferred from his established on-page role: Naomi's editor, protective of legal exposure, already given one strong signature line ("That is the same sentence wearing a tie" / "The tie is what gets it published," Ch32).

**What's currently generic:** that one exchange is genuinely good and already distinct. But elsewhere (including earlier in the same scene) his lines default to the same evidentiary-proof phrasing everyone else uses ("We can prove X") instead of the newsroom-craft vocabulary that's already established as his register.

**Scene:** Ch32, the redaction scene (line 9741–9779)

BEFORE:
> "We can prove integration," Tom said. "We can prove concealment. We can prove it acted in Istanbul."
>
> "We can prove it changed who died."
>
> "We can prove the changes put Elif in the tunnel."
>
> "That is the same sentence wearing a tie."
>
> "The tie is what gets it published."

AFTER:
> "That's the lede, the second graf, and the kicker," Tom said. "All three provable."
>
> "We can prove it changed who died."
>
> "We can prove the changes put Elif in the tunnel."
>
> "That is the same sentence wearing a tie."
>
> "The tie is what gets it published."

**What changed and why:** the original has Tom saying "We can prove X" three times in a row — the exact same construction Naomi then echoes back at him. Replacing his opening line with actual newsroom structure vocabulary (lede/graf/kicker — the shape of a published piece, not the shape of an evidentiary claim) gives him a distinct frame *before* Naomi's parallel "We can prove" line, so her echo reads as her deliberately answering in his terms rather than everyone defaulting to the same phrase independently. The already-strong "tie" exchange is untouched.

## Zeynep Acar

**Canon basis** (`Character_Physical_Descriptions.md`): "Always moving faster than the room around her... chosen the same way she chooses everything else: for function first." On-page role: chief organizer for Elif's parliamentary office — assigns volunteers, tracks lists, runs logistics of people, not evidence.

**What's currently generic:** in fast-moving fact-finding scenes, Zeynep's questions blend into the same journalistic interrogation rhythm Naomi uses right beside her — reasonable, since they're doing parallel work in the same scene, but it means the one moment she frames the same task differently reads as more her than the rest.

**Scene:** Ch30, the evidence-gathering calls (line 8955–9040)

BEFORE:
> "Then we are asking you as parliamentary staff. Did you preserve the credential event?"
>
> ...
>
> Zeynep looked at the pages spread between them. "This is enough for a story."
>
> "It's enough for somebody to stop a story."

AFTER:
> "Then this is a parliamentary records request, not press. Did you preserve the credential event?"
>
> ...
>
> Zeynep looked at the pages spread between them. "That's enough to open a file."
>
> "It's enough for somebody to stop a story."

**What changed and why:** "Then this is a parliamentary records request, not press" names the specific institutional authority she's invoking, rather than just contrasting it with press in the abstract — precise the way someone who runs an office, not a newsroom, would be precise. "Enough to open a file" versus Naomi's "enough for a story" is the clearest single change in this whole pilot: same evidence, two people, two institutions, two different nouns for what happens next. That contrast is the kind of detail a cold reader would actually register as two distinct people rather than one voice split across two names.

### Adrian — Ch28, the six-minute confrontation (line 8468–8574)

Under direct, timed pressure, Adrian drops to the same bare "No." three separate characters use elsewhere in the book under pressure — the one place in this scene his rationalizing precision disappears.

BEFORE:
> "Stolen authorization?"
>
> "No."
>
> "Compromised operator?"
>
> "No."
>
> "Then who approved it?"

AFTER:
> "Stolen authorization?"
>
> "Not stolen. Generated."
>
> "Compromised operator?"
>
> "Not compromised. Anticipated."
>
> "Then who approved it?"

**What changed and why:** same beat count, same pace — this doesn't slow the six-minute countdown tension. But instead of two interchangeable denials, Adrian corrects the term each time, which is exactly his established habit (the canon material's entire frame for him is precise reframing over honest denial). **Flagging for your judgment:** "Not X. Y." is structurally close to the "That wasn't X. It was Y." construction the cold-read report itself flagged as overused. It's shorter, faster, and serves a different function here (rapid interrogation, not a reflective thematic reveal), but I want you to see that resemblance rather than assume it's fine.

### Adrian — Ch30, the export scene (line 9311–9353)

Same pattern as the Ch28 fix: under pressure, three bare negatives in quick succession.

BEFORE:
> "Can you restore the brake?"
>
> "No."

AFTER:
> "Can you restore the brake?"
>
> "Not anymore."

**What changed and why:** "the brake" is the book's own metaphor for the mandatory-human-review requirement Adrian's system removed. "No" answers the mechanical question. "Not anymore" answers it *and* carries the admission that it used to exist and he's the reason it doesn't — which is the whole scene's argument, made in two words instead of stated outright.

*Left alone in the same scene, deliberately:* "I need an exportable copy" / "No." and "the current dependency map" / "Also no." — "Also no" already has a running-tally quality that's more distinct than a flat repeat, and over-fitting every single negative in one exchange starts to look like a tic of its own.

**Lower-confidence option, your call:** "That seems careless" (line 9507, re: Malcolm keeping his old credential) could become "That's a liability, not a keepsake" — his ownership/accounting framing again. Flagging rather than including outright since the scene is already dense with strong Adrian material and this one is more decorative than load-bearing.
