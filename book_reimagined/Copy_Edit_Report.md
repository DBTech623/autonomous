This manuscript is in strong copy condition. The developmental, cold-read, and line-edit work has left relatively little mechanical cleanup for a novel of this length and technical density. I do not recommend another prose-level rewrite before copy corrections are folded in.

### Working Copy Style Sheet

Recommended baseline: U.S. English, Chicago-style trade-fiction conventions, with a current U.S. dictionary as the spelling/compound authority. Where the manuscript has an internally consistent technical usage, consistency should generally outrank a generic dictionary preference.

- **Spelling:** U.S. forms are already consistent: gray, canceled, toward, defense, theater.
- **Serial comma:** Retain. The manuscript consistently uses the serial comma in narrative lists.
- **Numbers:** Spell ordinary narrative quantities where natural; retain numerals for exact clock times, vote totals, probabilities, record identifiers, page numbers, and technical values.
- **Percentages:** Use "percent" in prose; reserve the % symbol for interface/code material. Current usage is consistent.
- **Time:** Retain context-sensitive treatment: military/technical logs in 24-hour time; civilian narrative in 12-hour time where context supplies morning/afternoon; spoken times may remain spelled out.
- **Em dashes:** The manuscript consistently uses spaced em dashes in narrative and closed em dashes for interrupted dialogue. Treat this as project style unless a publisher later requires closed Chicago em dashes globally.
- **Display / interface language:** Backtick-delimited labels, records, prompts, and diagnostic strings are functioning consistently. Do not normalize apparent anomalies without checking narrative context.
- **Treaty naming:** Preserve the deliberate two-name system: "Eastern Mediterranean Framework" is the official name; "the Istanbul agreement" is the political/colloquial name.
- **Foreign names and places:** Retain diacritics: Rūta Vaitkutė, Klaipėda, Beşiktaş, Diyarbakır, Tunalı, Yalçın, etc.
- **Key technical forms:** Retain established project forms such as cross-domain, cross-border, failover, timestamp, cybersecurity, StratCore, NCP-7, POL-7, Project Polaris, and Aurora unless a publisher's house sheet supersedes them.

### Protected Intentional Anomalies — Do Not "Correct" Blindly

### What I Would Not Change in Copyedit

- The clipped fragments, procedural cadence, and technical vocabulary that create the thriller's voice. Those are line/style choices, not copy errors.
- The deliberate difference between official "Eastern Mediterranean Framework" usage and colloquial "Istanbul agreement" usage.
- NCP7 without a hyphen in the specific reimbursement record, because the text explicitly calls attention to that discrepancy.
- RESPONSIBILE AUTHORITY in Chapter 32, because the misspelling is intentional and immediately identified as Malcolm's.
- Technical labels that use all caps, slashes, arrows, or terse grammar. They are presented as interface/data artifacts and should not be forced into narrative grammar.
- The manuscript's strong preference for "said" and action beats over embellished dialogue tags; this is clean thriller practice and already consistent.
