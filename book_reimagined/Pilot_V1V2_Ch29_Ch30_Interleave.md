# Pilot Draft — V.1/V.2 (Ch29/Ch30 scene-level interleave)

**Purpose:** Test the third large structural bet flagged during the Ch25-32 verification: Ch28, 29, and 30 currently run as three consecutive single-POV chapters (Malcolm / Naomi / Malcolm) at exactly the moment the text itself asserts the two threads are simultaneous ("Naomi's first commitment entered while he watched," Ch30). All prose below is reused verbatim — nothing rewritten, only resequenced.

**Finding before the assembly, stated up front:** this pairing resists clean interleaving more than Ch20/Ch21 did. Ch20 and Ch21 were genuinely parallel threads with no content dependency in either direction. Ch30 is different — it doesn't just run alongside Ch29, it *consumes* Ch29's output. Malcolm's screen in Ch30 receives, almost line for line, the commitments Naomi generates in Ch29 (the magistrate's hold, the Brussels archive confirmation, the Greek liaison, the civil-rights attorneys). There is no split point where both halves can alternate without either showing an effect on Malcolm's screen before the reader has seen Naomi produce it, or degenerating into "run Ch29 whole, then Ch30 whole" — which isn't an interleave at all, just the chapter break removed.

I chose the first option below: split Ch29 at the one place a clean cut exists (the end of Naomi and Malcolm's phone call), and let Malcolm's screen show "Naomi's first commitment entered" before the reader sees Naomi actually produce it, revealed retroactively when the sequence cuts back. This is a real technique (effect before cause, then explained on cutback) rather than a mistake, but it's a trade-off, not a clean win, and it should be evaluated as such rather than assumed to work as well as the Ch20/21 pilot did.

## Read against the goal

- **The interleave technically works**, in that nothing above requires new prose and nothing violates causality outright — but it works by using effect-before-cause once (Malcolm receiving "Naomi's first commitment" and the Brussels reply before the reader watches her send them), not by true parallel alternation. That's a real technique, not an error, but it's doing something different than the Ch20/21 pilot did.
- **The back half barely interleaves at all.** After the split, Ch29's remainder (Naomi rewriting requests, first replies, "New paths are appearing") and Ch30's remainder (the confidence score climbing then falling as those same replies land) are each a single long uninterrupted block. The genuinely intercut portion is really just the front third; the back two-thirds reads close to the original two-chapters-in-a-row experience, just without the chapter break.
- **Honest recommendation:** this bet delivers less than the Ch20/21 one did, because the underlying chapters aren't actually parallel — Ch30 depends on Ch29's output. A lighter-touch alternative worth considering instead of a full interleave: keep Ch29 and Ch30 mostly sequential (as they already are), but open small windows into Malcolm's screen partway through Ch29 (or vice versa) at one or two points, rather than committing to a full scene-level swap that the underlying content doesn't actually support.
