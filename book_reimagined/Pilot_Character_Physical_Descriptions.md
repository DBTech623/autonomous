# Pilot Draft — Physical descriptions for 13 characters (14 insertion points), woven into their existing first appearances

**Purpose:** Phase 2 of the character-appearance fix (see `Character_Physical_Descriptions.md` for the approved Phase 1 bible). Every insertion below drops into a character's *existing* first on-page appearance rather than adding new scenes — the goal is texture, not new material. Each one either extends a sentence already doing descriptive work, or adds a short new sentence at a natural pause, matching the surrounding POV.

**POV discipline applied throughout:** A character can't casually narrate their own appearance in close third — so self-description (Malcolm, Naomi, Daniel) is anchored to a motivated device already present in the text (Malcolm's badge photo comparison, Naomi's external "entrance" framing, Daniel's external framing at the top of a new scene) rather than an invented mirror moment. Characters seen by someone else (Cate, Torres, Leila, Miles, Adrian, Elif, Zeynep, Voss) are described through the eyes of whichever POV character is already in the scene. Reddick, heard only on the phone in his one scene so far, is described as a mental image the POV character (Adrian) already has of him — not something newly seen.

## 1. Malcolm Carter (Ch5, badge-photo paragraph)

**After:**

A yellow stripe beneath the picture read `TEMPORARY ACCESS` in letters large enough to save everyone the trouble of asking.

## 14. Naomi Kincaid, cont. — Malcolm's first sight of her in person (Ch8, Union Market)

**Context:** This is a different beat from entry 7. Entry 7 is Naomi's introduction to the *reader*, in a scene Malcolm isn't in. This is Malcolm and Naomi's actual first in-person meeting, after their first encrypted call — the moment the user wants to carry the athletic build and disarming effect, filtered through Malcolm noticing and consciously not dwelling on it. Malcolm noticed it the way he noticed everything else that wasn't useful yet, filed it, and didn't look at it again.
>
> The tin had once held something called Himalayan Sunrise. Its lid was dented, and a faded price sticker covered part of a painted mountain.
