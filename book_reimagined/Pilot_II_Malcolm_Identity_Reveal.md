# Pilot Draft — Malcolm's identity/OSSI/Torres reveal to Naomi

**Purpose:** Fixes a real continuity gap surfaced during the Chapter 12 read-through. Malcolm withholds his last name, employer, and the existence of the audit from Naomi all the way through the Union Market meeting (Ch8) and the Vardonia election-night texts (Ch9) — consistently and deliberately ("Last name?" / "Later." / "Employer?" / "Also later." / "Government, then." / "You got there fast."). By Chapter 12 he's saying "It has not entered OSSI" and "I can tell Torres that a parliamentary record exists" as though she already knows both names, and she never blinks at either one. No scene between Ch7 and Ch12 shows the reveal. This is new material — nothing here is reused verbatim.

**Suggested placement:** New `* * *` section appended to the end of Chapter 10 ("The Cost of Correction"), after "The throttling order existed before the protest surge began." Same stretch of time as the rest of Ch10 — the night Luka's death is confirmed and the legal fight over the story plays out — before the "two days later" newsroom meeting that opens Chapter 12. Ch10 is otherwise entirely Naomi's POV with zero Malcolm content, so this keeps the chapter's existing rhythm (Malcolm/Naomi contact by phone, not in person, same as Ch8/Ch9) rather than requiring a new in-person scene this early.

**Craft notes:** Voice matches the established Malcolm/Naomi dynamic (Ch7-12), not the EW skill's default profile — close third, clipped dialogue, subtext through deflection, no explained emotion. The trigger is Luka's death, not curiosity: Malcolm has spent the whole book giving Naomi exactly as much as protects him. This is the first time he gives her something that costs him instead. OSSI is left unexpanded, matching every other instance in the manuscript. Torres's surname lands (needed for Ch12's casual use of the name); his first name is deliberately withheld and never needed again, since Naomi never uses it later either.

**This draft supersedes the first pass — the reveal now pays off two setups already in the book instead of treating the name as new information:** Chapter 5 ("The Redaction") has Naomi trace a scrubbed patent/grant record to "the third name," Malcolm Carter, and file it as "somebody with a record that stops on purpose" — meaning nothing yet, but saved beside the Vale amendment. Chapter 8 (Union Market) has him give only "Malcolm," and she "filed the coincidence where she kept things that weren't evidence yet." This call is where the last name closes that gap. She isn't hearing his name for the first time; she's confirming a suspicion she's been sitting on for weeks, and it's Malcolm's turn to be unsettled that her secrecy failed to hold up as well as his.

---

An encrypted-call request lit her screen. The account still read Malcolm, the only name she'd had for him since the market.

She let it ring twice, then accepted without video. Luka's last transfer still sat open on her second screen: a sending-device timestamp, ninety minutes of nothing, then silence that never resumed.

"If this is about the extract," she said, "I don't have anything new."

"It isn't."

Something in how fast he said it made her turn the volume up.

"I read what my own audit called Marin's death," Malcolm said. "An unresolved adverse consequence. Somebody wanted to fix the field name. Nobody did."

Naomi looked at Luka's frozen face, hand still raised toward the officer, waiting on an answer that had already stopped coming.

"Why are you telling me this."

Silence on the line. Not dead air. She'd have recognized dead air. This was the kind where someone was deciding something.

"My name is Malcolm Carter," he said.

Naomi didn't reach for the pen. She reached for the folder, the one with the Vale amendment paperclipped to a nine-year-old patent filing and a cached newsletter page nobody had thought to scrub. She'd written the name in it herself, three names down from two ordinary ones, and under it: *means nothing yet.*

"You're the redacted grant," she said. "Sponsored, restricted, no forwarding lab."

The pause on his end lasted longer than hers had.

"How much of that do you have?"

"Enough to have spent two nights confirming a date. You could have saved me the trouble a month ago."

"I didn't know you had a folder."

"It's the same folder as the Vale amendment. You've been in it since the market."

"I work for OSSI," he said, like he'd decided to keep going before she asked what that had to do with a fellowship that never sent a follow-up.

"Never heard of it."

"Most people haven't. That's what edited your grant record. It's also who's running the audit I mentioned."

Naomi set the pen down flat on the desk after all. "You're the audit."

"I'm on it. Someone else runs it."

"Who?"

"Torres."

"First name?"

"You don't need it yet."

The sound she made wasn't quite a laugh. "You made me chase half your name for a month. Now you hand me half of his too."

"I gave you what changed tonight."

"What changed?"

He didn't answer right away, and she let him not answer, watching the transfer log's dead progress bar instead.

"I had the seven minutes before it happened," Malcolm said. "You had the feed after. Between us we watched the whole thing, and neither of us could stop it."

"Does Torres know you're doing this?"

"No."

"Then why should I trust it?"

"You shouldn't. Verify it. The way you verified the grant."

She almost smiled at that. It was the first thing he'd said all night that didn't cost him anything to say.

"By morning I'll know more about OSSI than you'd like," she said.

"I'd be disappointed if you didn't."

The line stayed open a moment longer. She didn't hang up first.
