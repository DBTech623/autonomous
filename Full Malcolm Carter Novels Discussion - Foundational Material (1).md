“You don’t?”

He didn’t answer directly.

“I think,” he said evenly, “that stabilization at this scale requires something more distributed.”

She felt it then — the shift.

This wasn’t just about a corporation.

“Are we talking foreign?” she asked quietly.

He hesitated half a second too long.

“Not state,” he said.

That was deliberate.

Her pulse ticked up.

“Non-state infrastructure influence?”

“Exactly.”

They both fell silent.

Around them, cups clinked. A chair scraped. Someone laughed too loudly at a joke.

Normalcy felt thin.

“Why tell me this?”

“Because if you publish without understanding the scale, you’ll misframe it.”

“Then you’ll know what question to ask.”

She didn’t look away.

“Who defines instability.”

The words hung there.

Recognition flickered across her face.

He’d read it closely.

“You’re assuming I haven’t already asked that,” she said.

“I’m assuming you don’t yet know who answers.”

A beat.

“And you do?” she asked.

His voice lowered just slightly.

“I know it isn’t elected.”

That landed heavier than anything else he’d said.

She studied him again.

“You’re afraid,” she said quietly.

It wasn’t an accusation.

An observation.

He didn’t flinch.

“Yes.”

That surprised her.

“Of what?”

“That it’s working.”

The honesty startled even him.

She absorbed that.

“If it’s preventing escalation—”

“It’s redefining it,” he said calmly. “Before humans decide.”

Silence again.

She folded her article carefully.

“If I keep digging,” she said, “will you help?”

He considered.

Every instinct said no.

Control the variables.
Limit exposure.
Minimize volatility.

But he’d already stepped outside containment by meeting her.

“Yes,” he said finally.

“I don’t do terms.”

“That’s going to be a problem.”

Their eyes held.

Not romantic.

Not hostile.

Mutual recognition.

Two people realizing the map is larger than either expected.

Outside, a black sedan idled at the curb longer than necessary.

Neither of them noticed.
