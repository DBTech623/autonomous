# Autonomous

## Reader-Experience Pass 2 (post revision-plan)

**Status:** Diagnosis complete, no prose changes made yet
**Scope:** Full read of all 32 finalized v3 chapters, done fresh, after the revision plan's Passes 0–8

This is a second reader-experience pass, done after the developmental work that happened during the Codex phase (documented in `Autonomous_Reader_Experience_Pass.md`) and after this session's full pass through Chapters 25–32, the timeline check, the Polaris-trace verification, and the manuscript regeneration. The first pass fixed the book's worst problem: the early corrections had no human cost attached to them. That fix holds up on a fresh read — Chapter 1's Rūta sequence, Chapter 8's Luka's-death arc, Chapter 13's fire, Chapter 16's break-in, and the whole climax all land.

What's below is the problem that's left after that fix: **the book still runs on two different engines, and one is faster than the other.**

## The core finding

Every Naomi chapter has a body in the room — a physical stake she can see, touch, or lose. Every Malcolm chapter this side of Istanbul is, at bottom, people in a room arguing about what a sentence should say. The revision plan's Pass 4 gave each audit-room chapter a different *emotional verb* (Recognize, Contain, Risk, Defend...), and that helped — Chapter 6 now walks the floor instead of sitting at a table, Chapter 14 cuts to a hospital, Chapter 20 has Sam's grief and a real house around it. But verb-level differentiation doesn't fix a scene that is, underneath, still two or three people relitigating a definition. Your own reader notes flagged this directly on Chapters 1–6 ("the Naomi chapters are more interesting to me... I like Naomi's beats more so far"). Reading through 7–24 with that comment in mind, the pattern doesn't resolve — it just gets less dense per chapter.

This isn't a call for another incident-per-chapter pass like the first one. Adding a near-miss to every audit scene would flatten Polaris into a slasher villain. The fix is smaller: a small number of specific chapters where the talking has nothing else in it, and where a little physical or personal texture would cost almost nothing.

## Chapter-by-chapter notes

**Chapters 1–9:** Hold up well on reread. No new notes.

**Chapter 11 ("Acceptable Parameters")** — Two consecutive dialogue scenes: the `AUTONOMOUS`→`ANTICIPATORY` word fight, then the Naomi/Malcolm corridor scene. The word fight is thematically the point of the whole book (naming as an act of containment), so it earns its space — but it's the only scene in the chapter with zero physical grounding. A single sensory beat (the ventilation clicking off is already there and works; there's room for one more, maybe in the corridor scene) would be enough.

**Chapter 12 ("Contain the Language")** — The strongest procedural chapter craft-wise (Leila's cracker ritual, the transparent film sheets on the light table, Torres's hand-relabeling) but it's still two long conference-room debates back to back with no scene break in between doing anything but changing which four people are in the room. Consider whether the leadership meeting and the light-table correction session need to both happen in the same physical space, or whether moving one of them (a hallway, the parking structure, anywhere with a change of light) would help the chapter breathe.

**Chapter 17 ("Need to Know")** — Three rooms of talking in sequence: the finding-room debate, Cate's office, the elevator. The personal stakes (motive review, "put the denial in writing") are real and rising, which saves it, but it's the talkiest chapter in the book by volume. If one chapter in the whole manuscript could lose 150–200 words of debate without losing anything, this is the one — likely from the first finding-room scene, where the wording fight duplicates ground Chapter 11 already covered.

**Chapter 19 ("Scope")** — The weakest link in this stretch. It's a committee vote. The stakes are entirely institutional (Malcolm's appeal being declared moot before it's heard), which is a good idea, but the scene has no physical or sensory anchor at all — no object, no room detail, nothing for the reader's eye to land on between lines of dialogue. Chapters 5, 12, and 20 all have at least one physical prop doing quiet work (paper labels, crackers, the ceramic bowl of dead batteries). Chapter 19 has none. A small one would go a long way — even something as minor as what Malcolm does with his hands while five offices talk past him.

**Chapters 13–16, 18, 20–24:** Hold up well. Chapter 20 (Sam) in particular earns its two-hander format through the house, the cash box, the oven warranty — real objects instead of screens. Chapter 21's missed meeting and Chapter 23's suspension both have enough procedural tension of their own (a countdown, a locked door) that the talkiness reads as thriller pacing rather than drag.

**Chapters 25–32:** No notes. This is the strongest sustained stretch in the book — multiple POVs converging, physical stakes in every scene, and the procedural material (Cate's inquiry order, Adrian's boardroom) now moves fast because it's competing with a body count instead of a definition.

## A structural pattern worth naming, not necessarily fixing

Underneath the individual dialogue tics we already cleaned up (the "that wasn't the question" family), there's a larger recurring *scene shape* involving Cate, Torres, and Adrian: someone asks a direct question, gets an institutional deflection, and restates the question more sharply. We fixed the six-word version of this. The scene-length version of it is baked into Chapters 11, 12, 17, and 19 especially. I'm not recommending a rewrite of the pattern itself — it's doing real thematic work (it's the book's argument that institutions answer the question they're allowed to answer, not the one that was asked) — but four chapters in a row built on the same confrontation shape is likely part of why that stretch reads slower than the rest of the book, independent of any single line of dialogue.

## What I'd actually do, if asked

In priority order:

1. Trim Chapter 17's first scene by ~150–200 words (the debate ground it covers is already covered in Chapter 11).
2. Give Chapter 19 one physical anchor.
3. Consider a small setting change in Chapter 12 so its two debates don't read as one continuous scene.

All three are small, contained edits — nothing on the scale of the original reader-experience pass's new scenes. I haven't made any of them yet.

## What I'm deliberately not recommending

- No new incidents, near-misses, or action beats added to any Malcolm chapter. The first pass already did this exactly where the book needed it (Chapters 1, 3, 13, 14–16), and doing it again would cheapen Polaris into something that has to keep proving it's dangerous.
- No further work on the dialogue-tic family — that's fully swept (see this session's earlier work across 18 chapters).
- No changes to Chapters 25–32 — that stretch doesn't need this kind of note.
