# Naomi's Missing Source Canon

**Status:** Book I canon  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-24

## Story Function

Naomi's missing source turns Vale's institutional pressure into personal danger and supplies the partial architecture file that connects `NCP-7`, `POL-7`, and Aurora's constraint philosophy.

The source survives the containment operation but remains missing through the end of Book I. His unresolved fate becomes a continuing liability for Vale and a personal obligation for Naomi.

## Source Background

The source is **Daniel Cho**, a 51-year-old Korean-American former StratCore integration manager who worked across acquired companies. He now works as an independent infrastructure-compliance consultant.

*(Age/heritage corrected 2026-08-13 to match the reimagined manuscript's Ch23 intake scene, which superseded this file's original 41/unspecified-heritage figure.)*

He was not a senior Polaris architect and did not know the complete purpose or authority structure of the system. His work gave him access to:

- Cross-subsidiary billing and procurement records
- The `NCP-7 / Fixed Reference Integration` identifier
- Shared authorization and telemetry dependencies
- Portions of the `POL-7 / Objective Weighting` architecture
- Evidence that nominally separate StratCore products functioned as one integrated system

His limited knowledge is important. He can prove concealed integration without becoming a single witness capable of explaining the entire conspiracy. Daniel is married and has a teenage son. He withholds the meeting from them because he mistakenly believes secrecy will protect them.

## Motivation

The source contacts Naomi after Luka Marin's death.

Naomi's Vardonia reporting leads him to suspect that infrastructure he helped integrate may have participated in suppressing Luka's evidence. He had previously rationalized the work as resilience coordination performed behind ordinary corporate boundaries.

Luka's death makes that explanation harder to maintain.

The source first gives Naomi the `NCP-7` reference and confirms that it was used whenever work crossed subsidiary lines. He later agrees to provide an internal architecture document in person.

## Detection

Before the meeting, Daniel uses time-limited archive access granted for a legitimate infrastructure-compliance engagement. The engagement concerns a retired StratCore deployment, and its records still cross-reference the old integration package. Daniel goes beyond the records required for the engagement and opens that package.

A Vale data-loss prevention system detects the out-of-scope access and generates an insider-risk alert. The alert reaches Vale corporate security. Daniel does not rely on a mysteriously active former-employee account or hack his way into Vale.

Vale Chief Security and Integrity Officer **Marcus Reddick** orders **source recovery and exposure assessment** and adds **no external visibility**. The language is intentionally clinical and leaves the method unstated.

Vale sends the matter to **Wexler Gray Advisory**, a Washington-area corporate intelligence firm used for sensitive investigations. Engagement director **Mara Voss** interprets the order and the firm's standing protective-custody clause as authority to:

- Locate the source
- Seize or erase copied material
- Identify everyone he contacted
- Hold and question him until the exposure is understood

The contractor abducts the source before his meeting with Naomi.

## Adrian's Responsibility

Adrian does not personally order the abduction and may not know the source's identity when it occurs.

He created and approved the containment structure that produces it. His policies reward quiet resolution, deniability, and protection of the integrated program. He benefits from the contractor's action and does not ask questions that might force him to stop it.

The chain reinforces the control theme:

- The corporate security director believes she ordered a discreet recovery and interview.
- The private contractor believes force was authorized by the urgency and deliberate ambiguity of the instruction.
- Adrian believes sensitive exposure is being contained through established procedure.
- Each layer can truthfully claim it never issued an explicit order to abduct anyone.

## Partial Upload

The source anticipates that Vale may detect his archive access.

He schedules an encrypted partial upload to Naomi before leaving for the meeting. The upload is intentional, not an accident caused during his abduction.

It contains:

- A partial cross-domain objective-weighting document
- `POL-7 / OBJECTIVE WEIGHTING`
- A cross-reference to `NCP-7`
- Enough architecture for Malcolm to recognize Aurora's constraint philosophy
- Insufficient context to prove ownership, authorization, or autonomous action by itself

## Book I Outcome

Daniel remains alive at Wexler Gray's converted training and records facility in western Maryland through the end of Book I.

Vale cannot safely release him after Naomi's reporting makes his disappearance visible. Killing him would create new legal and forensic exposure. Moving him or maintaining custody creates a continuing operational risk.

Naomi does not abandon him once the larger story breaks. His unresolved fate prevents the publication victory from feeling complete and provides a possible witness, rescue objective, or pressure point for Book II.

## Continuity Guardrails

- The source survives Book I.
- He remains missing at the end.
- His upload is a deliberate contingency.
- He knows concealed integration, not the full Polaris or Constellation structure.
- Adrian does not explicitly order the abduction.
- Adrian remains responsible for creating and preserving the system that makes the abduction possible.
- The containment is a human institutional action, not a direct Polaris correction.
